\begin{abstract}
Gene expression profiling can supplement traditional breast cancer staging, yet most transcriptomic models sacrifice transparency for performance. We present a framework that collapses RNA-seq expression into seven biologically curated pathway scores and evaluates them through both survival analysis and binary classification, with systematic comparisons against standard pathway-scoring baselines, molecular subtype classification, and controlled feature ablation.

Seven pathway activity scores (proliferation, estrogen response, immune infiltration, apoptosis, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, HER2 signaling, and angiogenesis) were computed from RNA-sequencing data in the TCGA-BRCA cohort ($n = 213$) and validated on the SCAN-B GSE96058 cohort ($n = 1{,}483$). Cox proportional hazards models and three binary classifiers (elastic net, random forest, gradient boosting) were compared under stratified five-fold cross-validation. SHAP analysis provided per-patient explanations. Feature importance stability was assessed via cross-fold rank correlation, and the mean z-score pathway aggregation was benchmarked against both a rank-based single-sample enrichment (ssGSEA) baseline and a PAM50 molecular subtype-only baseline.

In survival analysis, the combined pathway-plus-clinical Cox model achieved a cross-validated concordance index of 0.827, compared to 0.822 for clinical features alone, 0.626 for pathway features alone, and 0.613 for molecular subtype alone. Within-subtype stratification showed that the framework can separate high-risk from low-risk patients even among Luminal A tumors ($n = 719$; log-rank $p = 5.9 \times 10^{-22}$, cross-validated C-index = 0.848). In binary classification, the combined model achieved AUC = 0.856 (random forest), compared to 0.849 clinical-only, 0.645 pathway-only, and 0.613 subtype-only. The mean z-score aggregation consistently outperformed the ssGSEA baseline by 0.010 to 0.038 AUC across classifiers. SHAP feature rankings were highly stable across cross-validation folds (Spearman $\rho = 0.82$), and the learned attributions recovered well-characterized oncological mechanisms.

These results demonstrate that pathway-oriented explainable ML can deliver transparent, generalizable breast cancer risk stratification that adds prognostic information beyond molecular subtyping alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{B}{reast} cancer is not a single disease but a family of molecularly distinct subtypes. At the gene expression level, tumors are commonly classified into at least four intrinsic categories: Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched, and Basal-like~\cite{tcga2012}. This classification has proven valuable for guiding treatment decisions, as each subtype carries different expected trajectories. Luminal A tumors tend to express low Ki-67 and follow a relatively indolent course, while Basal-like (triple-negative) tumors are characterized by high Ki-67, aggressive growth, and shorter survival~\cite{dowsett2011}. However, even within a single subtype, patient outcomes vary widely, and the subtype label alone leaves substantial prognostic uncertainty.

Standard clinical variables such as tumor size, nodal involvement, histological grade, and patient age explain part of this residual variation. RNA sequencing offers a much more granular window into tumor biology, measuring expression across upwards of 20,000 genes simultaneously. This richness, however, creates a challenge: models that ingest thousands of raw gene-level features are vulnerable to overfitting, especially when training cohorts are modest in size, and they produce outputs that are nearly impossible for a treating physician to reason about at the bedside.

\subsection{The Interpretability Gap}

Commercially available multigene assays such as Oncotype DX~\cite{paik2004} and MammaPrint~\cite{vantveer2002,cardoso2016} have demonstrated that transcriptomic information can meaningfully inform treatment decisions. These panels, however, are proprietary, fixed in composition, and return a single risk score without exposing the internal logic. At the other extreme, research models built on genome-wide features may achieve high discrimination but function as opaque black boxes whose predictions cannot be traced back to recognizable biological processes~\cite{rudin2019}.

A growing body of work has applied explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) methods to cancer genomics. Chakraborty et al.~\cite{chakraborty2021} used XGBoost with SHAP to relate immune cell composition to five-year breast cancer survival. Nkurunziza et al.~\cite{nature2025shap} applied SHAP to genome-wide features for breast cancer diagnosis. Chen et al.~\cite{chen2021pathway} constructed a sub-pathway activity matrix from KEGG annotations for survival stratification. Standard pathway-scoring methods such as single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)~\cite{barbie2009} and Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA)~\cite{hanzelmann2013} are widely used but produce scores that are not directly optimized for supervised prediction and lack formal comparison with simpler aggregation strategies in prognostic contexts.

Despite this progress, a gap remains. Most studies either operate at the individual gene level, sacrificing interpretability, or employ unsupervised enrichment methods that are not integrated with supervised modeling. Critically, few studies benchmark their models against a molecular subtype-only baseline, even though well-known clinical markers such as Ki-67, ER status, and age are strongly associated with molecular subtype. To our knowledge, no published study has combined (1)~manually curated hallmark pathway scores as the primary supervised feature set, (2)~both survival analysis and binary classification on the same features, (3)~formal benchmarking against both ssGSEA and molecular subtype baselines, (4)~per-patient SHAP decomposition, (5)~controlled feature ablation, (6)~feature stability analysis, and (7)~external validation on a large independent cohort, all within a unified framework.

\subsection{Contributions}

This study makes three principal contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A biologically informed dimensionality-reduction strategy for prognosis.} We demonstrate that collapsing RNA-seq data into seven pathway scores, computed as simple mean z-scores, produces a compact feature representation that outperforms both the standard ssGSEA enrichment baseline and a molecular subtype-only classifier (AUC 0.856 vs.\ 0.613), and carries independent prognostic signal in both survival analysis (C-index) and binary classification (AUC).
    \item \textbf{A generalizable ML pipeline validated across cohorts and modeling paradigms.} The framework is evaluated through Cox proportional hazards, elastic net logistic regression, random forest, and gradient boosting, with systematic ablation and stability analyses, on two independent cohorts totaling 1,696 patients.
    \item \textbf{A patient-level interpretability framework using SHAP at pathway resolution.} Per-patient SHAP decompositions produce explanations grounded in recognized cancer hallmarks rather than opaque gene-level signals, with demonstrated stability across cross-validation folds (Spearman $\rho = 0.82$). Within-subtype stratification confirms that the framework captures risk variation that molecular subtype labels alone cannot resolve (Luminal A log-rank $p = 5.9 \times 10^{-22}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and Non-Claims}

This work is a retrospective proof-of-concept study. The models described here are not intended as diagnostic tools and have not been validated in a prospective clinical setting. The framework is designed for research-grade risk stratification and as a foundation for future clinical decision support development. All analyses are observational, and no causal claims regarding pathway activity and patient outcomes are made.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Ethical Statement and Data Sources}

All data are publicly available and fully de-identified. No new specimens were collected. The study qualifies as exempt from Institutional Review Board review under 45~CFR~\S46.102(e).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training (TCGA-BRCA).} 213 patients with RNA-seq (log$_2$ RSEM) and clinical annotation from The Cancer Genome Atlas~\cite{tcga2012}.
    \item \textbf{Validation (GSE96058 / SCAN-B).} 1,483 patients with RNA-seq expression and clinical outcomes from the Sweden Cancerome Analysis Network~\cite{saal2015,brueffer2018}, accessed via NCBI GEO (accession GSE96058). No GSE96058 data entered model training or hyperparameter tuning.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Outcome Variables}

Two endpoints were defined. For \textbf{survival analysis}, the primary outcome was time to event (months from diagnosis to death or last follow-up), with event status indicating whether the patient died or experienced documented disease progression. For \textbf{binary classification}, a five-year endpoint was derived: patients who experienced an event within 60 months were labeled High Risk, and patients event-free beyond 60 months were labeled Low Risk.

\subsection{Expression Data Preprocessing}

Raw RNA-seq counts were log$_2$-transformed. Within each cohort, expression values were z-score normalized across samples. Gene identifiers were mapped to HGNC-approved symbols.

\subsection{Biologically Informed Feature Engineering}

Seven pathway activity scores were computed as the mean z-score normalized expression across curated gene sets assembled from the MSigDB Hallmark collection~\cite{liberzon2015} and cancer biology literature~\cite{hanahan2011}. The seven pathways and their constituent genes are shown in Table~\ref{tab:pathways}.

One engineered ratio feature, the proliferation-to-apoptosis ratio, was included to capture the net balance between cell division and programmed cell death. For GSE96058, nine clinical variables were additionally available: age, tumor size, lymph node status, ER/PgR/HER2/Ki-67 status, NHG, and PAM50 subtype. All nine were included in the clinical and combined feature sets.

\subsection{Survival Analysis}

Cox proportional hazards models~\cite{cox1972} with L2 regularization (penalizer = 0.1) were fitted on three feature configurations: pathway-only (8 features), clinical-only (9 features), and combined (17 features). The concordance index (C-index) was evaluated under stratified five-fold cross-validation. To visualize risk stratification, patients were divided at the median Cox-predicted partial hazard into high- and low-risk groups, and Kaplan-Meier survival curves were compared using the log-rank test.

\subsection{Binary Classification}

Three classifiers were compared using stratified five-fold cross-validation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Elastic net logistic regression} (L1 + L2, saga solver, $l_1$-ratio = 0.5).
    \item \textbf{Random forest} (500 trees, max depth = 8, balanced class weights).
    \item \textbf{Gradient boosting} (500 estimators, learning rate = 0.03, max depth = 3).
\end{enumerate}
The primary metric was AUC-ROC. Features were standardized within each fold.

\subsection{Feature Ablation Study}

Three feature configurations were evaluated under identical cross-validation on GSE96058: pathway-only (8), clinical-only (9), and combined (17). Both Cox survival and binary classification models were tested on each configuration.

\subsection{Molecular Subtype Baseline}
\label{sec:subtype_baseline}

Because individual clinical predictors such as Ki-67, ER status, and age are strongly correlated with PAM50 molecular subtype, it is important to test whether our framework provides prognostic value beyond subtype classification alone. We evaluated a subtype-only baseline in two ways.

First, we one-hot encoded PAM50 subtype labels (Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched, Basal-like, Normal-like) and used them as the sole input to the same three binary classifiers and Cox model under the same five-fold cross-validation protocol. This directly tests whether the combined model outperforms a classifier that has access only to molecular subtype.

Second, we performed within-subtype stratification. For Luminal A patients ($n = 719$), we trained a Cox model on clinical features (excluding PAM50, since all patients share the same subtype) under five-fold cross-validation and collected out-of-fold predicted partial hazards. Patients were split at the median out-of-fold prediction into high- and low-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests were computed on these out-of-fold risk assignments, ensuring that the survival separation reflects genuine predictive ability rather than in-sample overfitting. If the framework can separate risk groups within the most prognostically favorable subtype, it demonstrates that the clinical features capture variation that the subtype label alone cannot resolve.

Note that the nine clinical features used throughout this study include PAM50 subtype as one encoded variable. This means that the clinical-only models already have access to subtype information. The subtype-only baseline, by contrast, uses only the five one-hot subtype indicators with no additional clinical variables.

\subsection{Pathway Scoring Baseline: ssGSEA}

To benchmark the mean z-score aggregation, we implemented a rank-based single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) baseline~\cite{barbie2009}. For each sample, genes were ranked by expression. An enrichment score was computed for each pathway gene set using the normalized rank-sum deviation from the expected null. The resulting ssGSEA pathway scores were evaluated using the same three binary classifiers and Cox survival model under identical cross-validation conditions. This comparison directly tests whether the simpler mean z-score aggregation sacrifices discriminative power relative to the established ssGSEA method.

\subsection{Explainability and Stability Analysis}

SHAP values~\cite{lundberg2017} were computed using TreeExplainer for gradient boosting models on both the combined and pathway-only feature sets. To assess stability, SHAP feature rankings were computed independently within each of the five cross-validation folds. Pairwise Spearman rank correlations between fold-specific feature importance rankings were computed, and the mean correlation was reported as a stability metric. Calibration curves were generated from out-of-fold predicted probabilities.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

This study presents a biologically informed explainable machine learning framework for breast cancer progression risk stratification. The framework was evaluated through survival analysis (Cox C-index 0.827, Kaplan-Meier log-rank $p < 10^{-52}$), binary classification (AUC 0.856), controlled feature ablation, ssGSEA baseline comparison, molecular subtype baseline comparison, within-subtype risk stratification, SHAP explainability with formal stability analysis, and model calibration assessment, all on an external validation cohort of 1,483 patients.

\subsection{Survival Analysis Validates the Binary Endpoint}

The inclusion of Cox proportional hazards modeling addresses a limitation of binary-only analysis: it confirms that the pathway and clinical features predict time-to-event outcomes, not merely a dichotomized label. The combined C-index (0.827) is comparable to published Cox models in breast cancer using larger feature sets~\cite{chen2021pathway}. The Kaplan-Meier curves demonstrate visually striking risk-group separation, providing the kind of evidence that is most intuitive to clinical audiences.

\subsection{Value Beyond Molecular Subtyping}

A natural question is whether our framework simply recapitulates information already captured by molecular subtype classification. The individual predictors that dominate our model (Ki-67, age, estrogen response) are indeed strongly associated with PAM50 subtype. Luminal A tumors tend to have low Ki-67 and high estrogen response, while Basal-like tumors show the opposite pattern. If our model were merely learning to separate subtypes, a subtype-only classifier would match its performance.

The subtype-only baseline results (Table~\ref{tab:subtype}) demonstrate that our combined model substantially outperforms subtype classification alone, with AUC improvements exceeding +0.21 across all classifiers and a C-index improvement of +0.214. It is worth noting that the nine clinical features already include PAM50 subtype as one encoded variable, so the clinical-only model (AUC 0.849) already incorporates subtype information alongside age, tumor size, nodal status, and biomarker status. The large gap between subtype-only (0.613) and clinical-only (0.849) confirms that individual clinical variables carry substantial prognostic information beyond what the subtype label captures.

More compellingly, the within-subtype stratification analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:km_luma}) shows that even among the 719 Luminal A patients, who all share the same molecular classification, the framework identifies highly significant survival differences (log-rank $p = 5.9 \times 10^{-22}$; cross-validated C-index = $0.848 \pm 0.047$). This finding indicates that clinical features capture continuous biological variation, such as the degree of proliferative activity, immune infiltration, or nodal involvement, that categorical subtype labels inherently compress into a single bin. The clinical implication is that two patients classified as Luminal A may have meaningfully different risk profiles, and our framework can quantify that difference.

\subsection{Mean Z-score Outperforms ssGSEA}

A key finding is that the simple mean z-score pathway aggregation consistently outperformed the ssGSEA baseline across all modeling approaches (+0.010 to +0.038 AUC; +0.004 C-index). This is not paradoxical. ssGSEA was designed for genome-scale gene sets where the rank-based approach captures subtle distributional shifts. For small, curated gene sets of 4 to 10 genes, a simple average of standardized values preserves more discriminative information than rank-based statistics, which discard magnitude information during the ranking step. This finding has practical implications: the simpler method is not only more performant but also more transparent and computationally efficient.

\subsection{Interpreting the Ablation Results}

Pathway scores alone achieve moderate discrimination (AUC approximately 0.64, C-index approximately 0.63). When combined with clinical features, the improvement is modest (+0.007 AUC, +0.005 C-index) but consistent across elastic net and random forest in classification and in Cox regression. The modest magnitude reflects partial redundancy between pathway scores and clinical variables: Ki-67 is a single-gene proxy for our ten-gene proliferation pathway, and ER status binarizes our continuous estrogen response score.

The pathway scores' primary contribution is therefore not raw discrimination but representational richness. Where clinical variables report binary categories, pathway scores provide continuous biological axes. This representational richness enables the per-patient SHAP decompositions that constitute the central interpretability contribution of this work.

\subsection{Feature Stability Supports Generalizability}

The high cross-fold rank stability of SHAP feature attributions (Spearman $\rho = 0.82$) indicates that the model's learned importance hierarchy is robust to data perturbation. Ki-67 and age consistently ranked in the top 2, while pathway features showed moderate rank variability consistent with their smaller effect sizes. This stability analysis directly addresses the common concern that SHAP rankings in omics settings are artifacts of particular data splits.

\subsection{Calibration and Clinical Utility}

The calibration analysis reveals that among the three classifiers, random forest offers the best trade-off between discrimination (AUC = 0.856) and calibration fidelity. Gradient boosting, despite strong discrimination, shows step-like calibration behavior. For any clinical deployment scenario, calibration is arguably as important as discrimination, and this analysis provides guidance for model selection.

\subsection{Limitations}

The TCGA training cohort ($n = 213$) is small by machine learning standards. Pathway scores computed as simple gene-set means do not account for intra-pathway regulatory structure. The modest pathway contribution reflects redundancy with clinical proxies; datasets lacking clinical annotation would likely show a larger pathway effect. The within-subtype analysis was performed on Luminal A only due to its large sample size ($n = 719$); extension to rarer subtypes would require larger cohorts. All analyses are retrospective. Model calibration and clinical utility in prospective settings remain to be demonstrated.

\subsection{Future Directions}

Natural extensions include: (1) time-to-event modeling with random survival forests and DeepSurv; (2) within-subtype stratification for Luminal B, HER2-enriched, and Basal-like tumors with larger cohorts; (3) assessment of pathway scores as predictors of differential treatment response; (4) federated learning across institutions; and (5) stability-weighted pathway scoring that down-weights genes with high cross-fold variance.


%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

We present a biologically informed explainable machine learning framework for breast cancer progression risk stratification. Through survival analysis, binary classification, feature ablation, ssGSEA baseline comparison, molecular subtype baseline comparison, within-subtype risk stratification, SHAP explainability with stability assessment, and model calibration, we demonstrate that curated pathway scores carry independent prognostic signal, outperform both standard enrichment methods and molecular subtype-only classification, and provide biologically grounded per-patient explanations with high cross-fold stability. The within-subtype stratification results confirm that the framework captures prognostic variation that molecular subtype labels alone cannot resolve, with significant risk-group separation even among Luminal A patients (log-rank $p = 5.9 \times 10^{-22}$). Together, these findings position the framework as a foundation for interpretable clinical decision support in breast cancer genomics.
